Meet the LA

Hello, my name is Paisley Ivanovitch. I am a senior at the University of Montana, my major is Media Arts and my Minor is Japanese. I was the Learning Assistant for Japanese 202. I have been studying Japanese for 5 years and even lived in Japan for a year.

Why did you want to become an LA?

Well I was applying for the JET program which is a Japanese program where you assist teachers in teaching English to elementary, middle-school, and high-school students. I was talking to my Japanese professor about it and he was the one who suggested the LA program and really encouraged me to do it. I had no experience in teaching nor being an assistant teacher so I thought it would be a really good experience to have even if I didn't get into the JET program the first time around.

What were some challenges and successes you had during your time as an LA?

The biggest challenge for me was coming to a sudden realization of the knowledge I lack. I have been studying the language for a long time but I realized my speaking skills weren't as advanced so I couldn't explain things in Japanese like the professor could. I also sometimes had trouble answering the students' questions. I noticed the students often asked me questions more than they did the professor. I felt really happy that they felt comfortable enough to ask me but I couldn't answer some of them and they were all really good questions that would have been a great learning opportunity if they asked the professor instead. In those moments I would often direct the question towards the professor or ask the professor later so then I could go back and answer them. Luckily for me I had more successes than challenges. I met with my students for 50 minutes once a week outside of the classroom. I actually took Japanese 202 twice, once at UM and once again in Japan. I had a lot of personal experience, tips, and tricks that I was able to give the students that made the material easier to comprehend, and memorize.

How do you think your experience as an LA benefited you overall?

My experience as an LA really helped me grow inside and outside of the classroom. What we learned in the seminars made me feel a lot more confident when I applied them in the classroom. Even outside of the classroom I realized I could apply the same skills when helping my coworkers, and even my own professor. Now I will be applying the same skills overseas at a new job in Japan. I've also noticed I could apply the skills to myself as well and I found myself re-learning how to study, which is essentially just self-teaching. In turn I have noticed a huge improvement in my courses, my thinking, and my actions.

Would you recommend students join the LA program?

I would absolutely recommend the course to any and everyone. It is such a great experience even if you don't plan on going into a field related to teaching or student teaching. The skills you learn in the program can reshape everything you know in a way that really benefits you long term. Plus it's such a great feeling when you realize just how much you can help students, especially when you yourself have tips and tricks you learned that you now can pass down to them. I would also recommend the course simply because of how amazing the professor is. It was such a joy to work with him and to be in his class. For me his class felt like a stress relief class, like one of those classes you look forward to going to because you enjoy it so much. The professor, Mr. Herring, also has such a positive attitude and he never makes you feel like you're wrong or dumb. I think sometimes people don't realize just how much of a difference it can make in a course when the professor is as nice and caring as Mr. Herring is.
