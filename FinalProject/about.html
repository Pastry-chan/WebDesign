What is a
Learning Assistant?

Learning Assistants

Learning Assistants are students who come back to assist in a course they've already taken. LA's aren't responsible for teaching or grading material but instead they are there to help foster engagement, collaboration, and communication, and more in and out of the classroom. Their previous experience in the course gives them an advantage of being able to better identify student struggles and be able help meet their needs. LA's also have the ability to help instructors by providing feedback or giving suggestions on how they can improve the course. Being an LA provides a good opportunity for students to also reflect and develop their views on teaching and learning, for themselves and others.

Learning Assistants Program

The Learning Assistants program is a seminar that meets once a week for 50 minutes. During this seminar, students will have an opportunity to meet and interact with LA's from different courses and will work together to learn about different core concepts. The program aims to help students become more confident in their abilities, learn how to overcome obstacles and how to help students succeed. They also will learn how to develop an awareness of diversity within the learning environment and how to become more inclusive in their teaching practices. Learning Assistants earn up to 3 upper-division education credits depending on their LA commitments. For students in the Honors College, the LA experience can count towards elective or experiential learning requirements.

LA core strategies:

Wait Time

Wait time is a method where LA's wait about 3-5 seconds after asking students a question, or after a student responds. Wait time allows the students a moment to think over what is being asked of them or what their answer is. This improves learning by allowing breathing room for information to be processed, it boosts answer quality, and is known to increase participation.

Redirection

Redirection is used by LA's to help foster more participation or collaboration. Rather than answer a question directly, LA's might pose the question to another student or even a group which encourages more student interaction and allows an opportunity for active learning, and deeper thinking.

Understanding

Since LA's aren't teachers they can engage more with students in a peer-like manner which creates a safer feeling environment. This environment helps the students feel comfortable enough to express their troubles with the course to the LA. It is the LA's job to recognize and help aid in their understanding of the material, or help find solutions to their problems with the course. LA's will learn strategies on how to help recognize student misconceptions with the course material and how to help their understanding.

Collaboration

Collaboration is one of the most important skills. LA's learn how to foster collaboration through wait time, re-directing questions, and even helping students understand by allowing other students to help them. LA's also learn how to encourage more collaboration by creating safe feeling environments, as well as making it feel more personal by doing things such as making sure to learn everyones names, recognizing certain behaviors and learning how to adapt to meet everyones needs.
